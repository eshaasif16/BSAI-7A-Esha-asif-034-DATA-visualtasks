C.1  THE THREE CONDITIONS, TESTED IN CODE (slide 32)
==========================================================================
Condition 1: parts are exclusive.  2: the whole is meaningful.  3: the share is the question.

(a) the four touch_* columns of case08_channel_attrib.csv
    shares: {'touch_search': 42.62, 'touch_social': 32.3, 'touch_email': 27.48, 'touch_direct': 28.58}
    sum = 130.98%   exclusive = False   customers in 2+ channels = 1,105 of 4,000 (27.62%)
    1 exclusive parts ....... FAIL (shares sum to 131%, so a unit sits in several parts)
    2 meaningful whole ...... the whole is 'customers' - fine, but the parts are not slices of it
    3 share is the question . 'how many customers does each channel reach?' - that is four separate
                              percentages of the same 4,000, not one pie
    VERDICT: NOT a pie. A bar chart of reach per channel (each bar its own % of customers) is legal;
             a stacked bar or treemap is not, for the same reason as the pie.

(b) the eight rows of shares_eight.csv
    sum = 100.000000  (assert abs(sum - 100) < 1e-6 passes)
    every SKU belongs to exactly one sub-category: True
    the three middle slices are within 3.8 points (C 15.0%, H 13.1%, G 11.2%)
    1 exclusive parts ....... PASS   2 meaningful whole ... PASS (the eight are all the sub-categories,
    so the whole is the whole catalogue)   3 share is the question ... PARTLY: the real question is a
    ranking, and eight slices is twice the 4 +/- 1 chunks a reader holds.
    VERDICT: legal as a pie, but draw it as a sorted bar chart. Part C.2-C.3 tests exactly that.

(c) premium vs other revenue, case09, 2025Q4
    premium 228.0 + other 372.0 = 600.0  ->  38.0% + 62.0% = 100.0%
    1 exclusive parts ....... PASS (other is defined as total minus premium)
    2 meaningful whole ...... PASS for ONE quarter (all revenue in 2025Q4)
    3 share is the question . PASS only if the question is 'how much of this quarter is premium'.
    It FAILS the moment the question is 'is premium growing?': the whole shrank 1,000 -> 600 while premium moved 240 -> 228.
    VERDICT: a two-slice pie (or one stacked bar) is legal for the single quarter, and a bar is
             clearer; NEITHER may be used to show a change over time.

  pie: 2 of 2 wrong ranks are in the predicted middle (ranks 3-5); outside it: none.
  bars: no errors, so the prediction cannot be tested on this stimulus.

Time: pie 25.7 s, bars 9.1 s (bars faster, 16.6 s apart).

What one reader can and cannot support: this is a single person, tested once on each chart, always pie
first and bars second. The bars may be faster partly because the reader had just seen the same eight
letters (a practice effect I could not counterbalance with one reader), and one person's speed and errors
carry no information about how much they vary between people. It can illustrate the deck's claim; it
cannot confirm it, and it does not measure how large any pie-versus-bar difference is.

C.4  MY RULE. I will draw a pie only when there are two to four exclusive parts of a whole the reader cares about and the question is 'how much of the whole is this one part'; for a ranking, for five or more parts, or for anything that changes over time, I will draw sorted bars.
