The six faults and the rule applied to each
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. TWO TIMESTAMP FORMATS. 9,019 rows are ISO (2024-04-29 11:00:00),
   3,651 are dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm. Day-first PROVED: 2,151 slash rows have a first field above 12,
   0 have a second field above 12. So the slash rows are day-first.
   Rule: parse each format with its own explicit format string.
   pd.to_datetime(format='mixed') would also parse every row and lose none,
   but it reads the ambiguous slash dates month-first: the series would run 2024-01-04 -> 2025-12-08
   instead of the true 2024-04-01 -> 2025-09-11, and move 1,382 rows to the wrong day.
2. NOT SORTED. Sorted on arrival: False. Rule: sort_index() once, after parsing.
3. DUPLICATE HOURS. 286 rows repeat an hour already present (one in one format, one in the other).
   All 286 are identical in every value (0 conflicting). Rule: drop the repeat, keep the first.
4. DEVICE SPELLED SIX WAYS (three devices): {'Mobile': 6994, 'Desktop': 3452, 'mobile': 883, 'Tablet': 777, 'desktop': 318, 'MOBILE ': 246}
   Rule: strip + lower-case -> {'mobile': 7942, 'desktop': 3681, 'tablet': 761}.
5. SENTINEL -1 in sessions: 290 raw rows (287 after de-duplication, on 222 different days).
   Rule: set to NaN, never to 0. Mean sessions/hour moved 946.94 -> 969.43.
   Daily sums are made from the hours that were recorded; 222 days have 1-3 dropped hours, so those days read a
   little low (one hour is ~4% of a day). Not filled; flagged here instead.
6. MISSING PERIODS. Counted on the HOURLY index before resampling: 12,696 hours expected, 12,384 present,
   312 missing = 13 whole days (every one of them all 24 hours: True).
   Days: 2024-05-18, 2024-07-02, 2024-07-03, 2024-09-27, 2024-11-11, 2024-12-25, 2025-01-01, 2025-02-14, 2025-03-30, 2025-03-31, 2025-06-07, 2025-07-19, 2025-08-23
   Rule: reindex to a complete calendar; those days stay NaN and the line breaks.
   (resample('D') alone would NOT have shown them - it invents the rows.)
