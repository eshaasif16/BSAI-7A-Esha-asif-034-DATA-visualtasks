B1  The size range (health_indicators.csv)
==========================================================================
I use area = 12 pt^2 per million people, i.e. sizes=(7.2, 4,241) in points^2.
  (matplotlib's s is the marker's diameter squared, so diameter = sqrt(s) points; area is proportional to s.)
  smallest mark: 0.6 M -> 7.2 pt^2 -> diameter 2.68 pt = 0.95 mm. About 1 mm is the smallest dot I trust to survive print.
  largest mark : 353.4 M -> 4,241 pt^2 -> diameter 65.1 pt = 0.90 in
  population ratio 589x -> AREA ratio 589x -> DIAMETER ratio 24.3x  (= sqrt of 589)
  area vs radius: if population drove the RADIUS the diameter ratio would be 589x and the area ratio 346,921x. I used area, so it is 24.3x.
  Lie Factor of the size channel (ink = area): 1.00 -> honest
  swallow test: centres of other marks inside the largest bubble (Wessant): 8 ['Belmara', 'Tevrany', 'Xanthos', 'Ystrad', 'Greyholm', 'Quenvar', 'Yelvora', 'Gorlanth']
  candidate scales (area = k pt^2 per million), smallest dot vs neighbours under the biggest bubble:
    k= 8: smallest dot 0.77 mm, largest 0.74 in, 7 other centres inside the largest bubble
    k=10: smallest dot 0.86 mm, largest 0.83 in, 7 other centres inside the largest bubble
    k=12: smallest dot 0.95 mm, largest 0.90 in, 8 other centres inside the largest bubble
    k=14: smallest dot 1.02 mm, largest 0.98 in, 9 other centres inside the largest bubble
    k=16: smallest dot 1.09 mm, largest 1.04 in, 14 other centres inside the largest bubble
  I keep k=12: it is the smallest k whose 0.6 M dot is ~1 mm across, and the biggest bubble stays under an inch. The largest bubble does overlap neighbours (alpha 0.62 keeps them visible), and the size legend carries real populations.

four northernmost districts (by mean vertex latitude): ['D21', 'D22', 'D19', 'D24']
  share of total area, spherical (correct) : 10.51%
  share of total area, shoelace on degrees : 11.08%  (over-states by +5.5%)
most distorted single district: D21 (Ushkan) at lat 35.0: true share 2.15%, degree-space share 2.27% (+5.6%)
Mercator area inflation (projected area / true area): min 1.237 (south) .. max 1.492 (north); whole federation 1.340x
  so the north is drawn 20.5% larger than the south for the same real land.
  (Mercator's AREA factor is sec^2(latitude): sec^2(24 deg)=1.20, sec^2(36 deg)=1.53. geolib's docstring says sec(lat), which is the LINEAR scale factor, so I measured the areas directly instead of trusting either.)
Why that number matters: a choropleth encodes the value in colour AND in the district's drawn area. Mercator draws every district 1.24-1.49x too big and the north 21% bigger than the south, so the northern districts shout louder for the same real land. Only an equal-area projection keeps the drawn area honest. (slide 34)

B5  Small-area stability (smallarea_cases.csv)
==========================================================================
pooled rate 8.72 per 100,000 from 259 cases / 2,970,500 people, 40 units
units outside the 95% Poisson funnel: 1 of 40 -> ['U32']
  by chance alone about 5% of 40 = 2 would fall outside, so 1 is fewer than noise alone would give - but the one that does is U32 itself, so I also ran the exact test:
  exact Poisson check on U32: P(>=9 cases | expected 1.92) = 0.00018; across 40 units ~0.007. The data's own dictionary says the true rate is the same everywhere, so U32 is a rare tail draw, not a hotspot.
what it tells me about the case 6 map: the extreme ranks are filled by the smallest populations. Of the 10 units furthest from 8.72, 8 have fewer than 50,000 people (the median population of all 40 is 50,000, so about 5 would be expected if size did not matter). The rank correlation between population and distance from the pooled rate is only -0.15, because many small units are also close to the pooled rate; the signature of small numbers is the SPREAD, not the average distance: the standard deviation of the rates is 10.3 for the 13 units under 30,000 people against 2.0 for the 10 units over 100,000. The units over 100,000 all sit between 5.5 and 12.0, while the units under 30,000 range from 0.0 to 40.9.
minimum population to tell 9 from 18 per 100,000:
  one unit against a KNOWN rate of 9 (18 outside its 95% upper limit): N >= 42,684
  two units, each with its own sampling error (the realistic case):   N >= 128,053 each
  8 of the 40 units reach the second threshold; 21 reach the first.
